1. WHAT IS IKIGAII?
================================================================================

Ikigaii is a systematic, rules-based monthly-rebalancing equity strategy that:
  - Ranks 138 NSE stocks every month using 3 quantitative factors
  - Picks the top N stocks (equal weight, 1/N each)
  - Uses a market regime filter to decide if we should be in equities at all
  - When risk-off, allocates to Gold or Cash (whichever has better momentum)
  - Applies a daily trailing stop-loss to limit drawdowns
  - Caps sector concentration to avoid blow-ups

It evolved from the Giordano (2018) Charles H. Dow Award paper through several
iterations: base RAAM -> ablation studies -> enhanced engine -> final Ikigaii.


================================================================================
2. ANCESTRY & MOTIVATION
================================================================================

STEP 1: GIORDANO (2018) - "Relative & Absolute Momentum"
---------------------------------------------------------
The academic paper proposed a 4-pillar ranking system for a 12-ETF cross-asset
universe:
  (M) Momentum       - rate of change
  (V) Volatility     - EWMA RiskMetrics vol
  (C) Correlation    - avg pairwise correlation (diversification)
  (T) ATR Trend      - breakout/trend signal

Total Rank = wM*Rank(M) + wV*Rank(V) + wC*Rank(C) - T + M/x
Lower rank = better asset. Top-N selected monthly, equal weight.

STEP 2: RAAM (Ranked Asset Allocation Model)
---------------------------------------------
We adapted Giordano to the Indian equity universe (138 NSE stocks).

Key adaptation: DROPPED the Correlation (C) pillar.
WHY: In a single-country equity universe, low-correlation stocks are just
illiquid oddities, not genuine diversifiers. On a 12-ETF cross-asset universe
(US bonds, commodities, international equity) correlation ranking identifies
true diversification. On 138 Indian stocks, it just picks illiquid garbage.
This was validated empirically - correlation ranking HURT performance.

Remaining 3 pillars (M, V, T) with renormalized weights:
  wM/(wM+wV) and wV/(wM+wV) for momentum and volatility ranks.

STEP 3: GSADF V10 (Parallel Development)
------------------------------------------
A separate strategy (GSADF V10) was developed using GSADF bubble detection +
RSI + TEIS scoring. It served as:
  - Source of the GSADF cache (pre-computed GSADF signals per stock)
  - Benchmark to beat
  - Data pipeline (parquet loader, price data, metrics module)

V10 stats: CAGR=22.0%, Sharpe=1.01, MDD=-22.3%

STEP 4: IKIGAII (Final System)
-------------------------------
Combined the best elements:
  - RAAM momentum + volatility ranking (from Giordano)
  - GSADF signals from V10 as a third factor (replacing correlation)
  - Nifty trend regime gate (from ablation study)
  - Momentum-pick Gold/Cash risk-off (new addition)
  - Daily trailing stop-loss (from enhanced engine ablation)
  - Sector concentration caps (from enhanced engine)
  - Survivorship bias fix (delisted tickers added)

3. THE RANKING ENGINE (3 PILLARS)
================================================================================

Every trading day, each stock gets a composite rank. Lower = better.

PILLAR 1: MOMENTUM (M) - Weight wM (highest)
----------------------------------------------
What: Rate of change over a lookback window (MOM_WINDOW days).
How:  prices.pct_change(MOM_WINDOW).shift(1)
      The .shift(1) means row i uses only prices through day i-1.

Ranking: Cross-sectional rank, ascending=False (highest momentum = rank 1).

Why momentum: Momentum is the most robust factor in equity markets. Stocks
that have gone up tend to keep going up (intermediate-term). Giordano used
it as the primary signal with the highest weight.


PILLAR 2: VOLUME CONFIRMATION (VolConf) - Weight wVC
------------------------------------------------------
What: Short-term volume average / Long-term volume average.
How:  (volume.rolling(VOL_SHORT).mean() / volume.rolling(VOL_LONG).mean()).shift(1)

Ranking: Cross-sectional rank, ascending=False (higher ratio = rank 1).

Why VolConf: This replaced the original EWMA Volatility pillar from Giordano.
Volume confirmation measures whether recent trading volume is elevated vs its
norm. Rising volume confirms momentum - a stock going up on increasing volume
is more convincing than one on declining volume.

The original Giordano volatility pillar (EWMA RiskMetrics vol, rank low vol
as best) was designed for cross-asset allocation where you want to overweight
low-vol assets for risk parity. On a single-country equity universe, VolConf
provides more alpha because it captures institutional interest and conviction.


PILLAR 3: GSADF SIGNAL - Weight wG
--------------------------------------
What: Generalized Sup ADF test statistic (pre-computed, cached).
How:  Read from gsadf_cache.parquet (computed by GSADF V10 pipeline).

The GSADF test detects explosive price behavior / bubble formation.
Higher GSADF = more "explosive" upward price behavior.

Ranking: Cross-sectional rank, ascending=False (highest GSADF = rank 1).

Why GSADF: This was the key innovation from the V10 strategy. GSADF captures
a different alpha source than momentum - it detects parabolic price moves early.
Combined with momentum and volume, it provides three orthogonal views:
  - Momentum: "has it been going up?"
  - VolConf: "is the move backed by volume?"
  - GSADF: "is the move accelerating explosively?"


COMPOSITE RANK FORMULA
-----------------------
  r_mom    = mom.rank(axis=1, ascending=False, method="min")
  r_vc     = vol_conf.rank(axis=1, ascending=False, method="min")
  r_gsadf  = gsadf_panel.rank(axis=1, ascending=False, method="min")

  total_rank = wM * r_mom + wVC * r_vc + wG * r_gsadf + mom / TIEBREAKER_DIVISOR

The "+ mom / TIEBREAKER_DIVISOR" is a TIEBREAKER term. When two stocks have
identical weighted ranks, raw momentum breaks the tie. The divisor is large
enough to make the tiebreaker negligible vs the main rank terms but
deterministic.


================================================================================
4. THE REGIME GATE (MARKET FILTER)
================================================================================

WHAT IT DOES
-------------
Before allocating to equities, we check: "Is the broad market in an uptrend?"
If NO -> go fully risk-off (Gold or Cash). If YES -> select top-N stocks.

THE CHOSEN GATE: Nifty SMA Trend Filter
-----------------------------------------
  sma = benchmark_close.rolling(SMA_WINDOW).mean().shift(1)
  regime = (benchmark_close.shift(1) >= sma).astype(float)

  - If Nifty (previous day close) >= its SMA -> RISK ON (1.0)
  - If Nifty (previous day close) < its SMA  -> RISK OFF (0.0)
  - The .shift(1) on both price and SMA ensures no lookahead

WHY THIS GATE (THE ABLATION STORY)
------------------------------------
We tested 8 different regime detectors plus combinations. Here is what we found:

  Detector         | Sharpe | MDD     | Risk-Off% | Verdict
  -----------------+--------+---------+-----------+------------------
  none (no gate)   | 0.62   | -35.1%  |  0.0%     | Baseline
  hurst            | 0.64   | -35.1%  |  1.1%     | Barely different
  absorption       | 0.62   | -35.1%  |  3.7%     | Negligible effect
  garch_vol        | 0.58   | -33.3%  | 21.3%     | Sacrifices CAGR
  yz_vol           | 0.57   | -29.8%  | 19.2%     | Moderate help
  nifty_trend      | 0.50   | -23.5%  | 26.9%     | BEST MDD protection
  cusum            | 0.42   | -32.5%  | 13.1%     | Worst of both worlds
  breadth          | 0.43   | -35.1%  |  8.8%     | Ineffective
  turbulence       | 0.49   | -35.1%  | 11.6%     | Rarely triggers

We also tested combinations:
  AND(hurst,absorption,garch_vol) | 0.60 | -33.3% | 25.8%
  MAJ(hurst,absorption,garch_vol) | 0.62 | -35.1% |  0.3%

KEY INSIGHT: In the base RAAM ranking-only system, nifty_trend had the lowest
Sharpe but the BEST max drawdown (-23.5% vs -35.1% baseline). The Sharpe
dropped because being risk-off ~27% of the time costs CAGR. But when combined
with the enhanced ranking (VolConf + GSADF) and trailing stops, the MDD
protection compounded into the best overall risk-adjusted result.

The final Ikigaii with nifty_trend gate achieved: Sharpe=1.35, MDD=-33.8%.
The gate alone was not enough for MDD - the trailing stops did the rest.


================================================================================
5. THE RISK-OFF MECHANISM (GOLD vs CASH)
================================================================================

WHAT HAPPENS WHEN RISK-OFF
----------------------------
When the regime gate says "risk off," we do not just sit in cash. We pick
between Gold (GoldBees) and Cash (LiquidBees) based on which has better
trailing momentum.

HOW IT WORKS
-------------
  gold_mom   = gold_px.pct_change(GOLD_MOM_WINDOW).shift(1)
  liquid_mom = liquid_px.pct_change(GOLD_MOM_WINDOW).shift(1)

  Each day in risk-off:
    if gold_mom > liquid_mom -> earn Gold daily return
    else -> earn LiquidBees daily return

WHY
----
During equity bear markets, gold often rallies (flight to safety). Sitting in
pure cash (LiquidBees) is safe but leaves money on the table. Momentum-picking
between gold and cash captures the gold rally during crises while defaulting
to cash when gold is also declining.

The lookback window (GOLD_MOM_WINDOW) was chosen to match the typical length
of a market correction (roughly a quarter). Shorter lookbacks flip too often,
longer ones are too slow to react.


================================================================================
6. TRAILING STOP-LOSS SYSTEM
================================================================================

THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES
----------------------
Monthly rebalancing means you hold positions for ~21 trading days between
rebalances. A lot can go wrong in a month. A stock can crash hard intraday
and you are stuck until month-end.

THE MECHANISM
--------------
  Stop Level: -STOP_PCT% trailing from peak

  For each held position, every day:
    1. Update the peak price using YESTERDAY high (shifted, no lookahead)
       if yesterday_high > current_peak: peak = yesterday_high
    2. Check stop using YESTERDAY low (shifted, no lookahead)
       if yesterday_low < peak * (1 - STOP_PCT): STOPPED OUT
    3. Stopped capital moves to LiquidBees until next monthly rebal
    4. At next rebal, the stopped slot reopens for fresh ranking selection

WHY THIS STOP LEVEL (THE ABLATION)
-------------------------------------
We tested multiple stop levels from tight to loose, plus no stop at all.

Key findings from the ablation:

  Tighter stops:
    - Fewer large losses per position
    - But trigger too often (hundreds of stops), whipsaw in volatile markets
    - Costs CAGR from forced exits + re-entry transaction costs

  Looser stops:
    - Higher Sharpe and CAGR (fewer false triggers)
    - But MDD approaches no-stop levels (diminishing protection)

  No stop at all:
    - Worst month was extreme (>-30%). MDD was >-44%. Unacceptable.
    - Single-stock crashes propagate fully into the portfolio

  The chosen STOP_PCT is the sweet spot:
    - Moderate number of stops (reasonable turnover)
    - Meaningful MDD protection vs no-stop
    - Worst month stays manageable
    - Calmar ratio (CAGR/MDD) is strong

Note: The final Ikigaii numbers differ slightly from the enhanced ablation
because Ikigaii uses the nifty_trend gate + gold/cash risk-off, which
further reduces MDD.

7. SECTOR CAPS
================================================================================

RULE: Maximum MAX_SEC stocks per sector at any rebalance.

HOW IT WORKS
--------------
  When selecting top-N from rankings:
    1. Walk down the ranked list from best to worst
    2. Check the sector of each stock
    3. If that sector already has MAX_SEC stocks selected, SKIP it
    4. Continue until N stocks are selected or list is exhausted

SECTOR CLASSIFICATION
----------------------
138 stocks are classified into sectors:
  IT, Banks, Pharma, FMCG, Auto, Capital Goods, Metals, Financial Services,
  Oil and Gas, Power, Telecom, Infrastructure, Chemicals, Cement, Conglomerates,
  Real Estate

WHY
----
Without sector caps, the strategy would sometimes load all N positions from
the same sector because they move together. When the sector corrects, all
positions drop simultaneously. Capping at MAX_SEC forces diversification
across sectors, reducing tail risk from sector-specific events.

The cap was chosen to be:
  - Not too restrictive (too low blocks valid concentrated bets)
  - Not too loose (too high allows the strategy to pile into one sector)
  - Balanced: allows meaningful sector bets while preventing full concentration


================================================================================
8. SURVIVORSHIP BIAS FIX
================================================================================

THE PROBLEM
------------
If you only backtest on stocks that exist TODAY, you are cheating. Stocks that
went bankrupt, got delisted, or crashed to zero are excluded from the universe,
making your backtest look artificially good.

THE FIX
--------
We added delisted/distressed tickers to the universe:
  RCOM, YESBANK, JETAIRWAYS, JPASSOCIAT, UNITECH, RPOWER, HDIL, DEN,
  PCJEWELLER, IBREALEST, DISHTV, IDEA, COFFEEDAY, RELCAPITAL, RELINFRA

Each has its sector assigned (YESBANK=Banks, JETAIRWAYS=Infrastructure, etc.)
and its historical price data loaded from parquet files.

If the strategy would have bought YESBANK in 2018 (before its collapse), it
should show that loss. Without these tickers, the backtest would never have
"seen" the risk of holding a bank that goes to near-zero.

These tickers are loaded if they have sufficient bar history. They naturally
"die" from the universe when their price data stops (delisting date).


================================================================================
9. EXECUTION MODEL
================================================================================

REBALANCING
-----------
  Frequency: Monthly (last trading day of each month)
  Method:    Full re-ranking + top-N selection + equal weight
  Warmup:    No rebalancing until sufficient bars for indicator computation

POSITION SIZING
---------------
  Equal weight: 1/N per position
  When fewer than N positions (due to sector caps or sparse rankings),
  the remainder goes to risk-off allocation.

TRANSACTION COSTS
-----------------
  Cost: COST_BPS basis points per unit of turnover
  Turnover = sum of absolute weight changes across all positions
  Applied BEFORE updating equity (conservative)

  This cost estimate covers Indian market frictions:
    - Brokerage (discount broker)
    - STT (securities transaction tax)
    - Impact cost (for Nifty 200 stocks)
    - Stamp duty + other charges

CAPITAL FLOW
-------------
  Stock positions -> monthly from ranking
  Risk-off weight -> goes to gold/cash momentum pick
  Stopped capital -> goes to LiquidBees until next rebal
  At rebal: LiquidBees buffer resets, full re-ranking occurs

10. LOOKAHEAD PREVENTION (ZERO DATA LEAKAGE)
================================================================================

Every single indicator is shifted by 1 day. This means:

  Row i (today) only uses data available through day i-1 (yesterday).

Specifically:
  - Momentum:     prices.pct_change(MOM_WINDOW).shift(1)
  - VolConf:      volume_ratio.shift(1)
  - GSADF:        pre-computed, applied with reindex (no future data)
  - Regime gate:  benchmark.shift(1) >= sma.shift(1)
  - Trailing stop peaks: updated using highs.shift(1), checked using lows.shift(1)
  - Gold/Cash mom: pct_change(GOLD_MOM_WINDOW).shift(1)

WHY SHIFT 1 DAY?
  In live trading, you compute indicators after market close. You can only
  act on them the NEXT day at open. The 1-day shift simulates this reality.
  Without it, you would be using today close to make today decision, which
  is impossible in practice (you cannot see the close before market closes).

THE OHLC SHIFT FOR STOPS
  The trailing stop uses yesterday HIGH to update the peak and yesterday
  LOW to check the stop. This is critical - using today intraday data would
  be lookahead. We shift the entire high/low panels by 1 day at data prep:
    highs_shifted = highs.shift(1)
    lows_shifted  = lows.shift(1)

  The highs/lows themselves are also forward-filled (with a small limit)
  then filled with close prices if missing, to handle gaps in OHLC data.


================================================================================
11. DATA PIPELINE
================================================================================

SOURCE
------
  yfinance auto-adjusted prices, stored as per-ticker parquet files.
  Location: gsadf_v10/data/{TICKER}.NS.parquet
  Each parquet has columns: open, high, low, close, volume

LOADER (gsadf_v10/src/loader.py)
----------------------------------
  1. Read close prices for all 138 universe tickers
  2. Forward-fill short gaps
  3. Drop tickers with insufficient history (below MIN_BARS)
  4. Load LIQUIDBEES.NS returns (risk-off proxy)
  5. Load ^NSEI (Nifty 50 benchmark)
  Returns: prices DataFrame, risk_off_rets Series, nifty_close Series

OHLC LOADING (ikigaii.py prep_data)
-------------------------------------
  1. For each stock, load high/low/volume from its parquet
  2. Forward-fill short gaps, fill remaining NaN with close
  3. Shift highs and lows by 1 day for lookahead-free stop checks

GSADF CACHE
-----------
  Pre-computed by GSADF V10 pipeline, stored at:
    gsadf_v10/outputs/gsadf_cache.parquet
  This is a panel (dates x stocks) of GSADF test statistics.
  Computing GSADF from scratch is extremely expensive (rolling ADF tests
  with expanding windows), so it is cached once and reused.

12. THE ABLATION JOURNEY (WHAT WE TESTED & WHY)
================================================================================

The development was highly empirical. We did not just pick parameters out of
thin air - every design choice was ablated (tested in isolation) and validated
across train/test/holdout splits.

PHASE 1: BASE RAAM vs GSADF V10
---------------------------------
First test: does the Giordano ranking approach (M+V+T) work on NSE stocks?

  Base RAAM (M+V+T, no crash gate):
    CAGR=15.9%, Sharpe=0.64, MDD=-35.8%

  GSADF V10 (GSADF+RSI+TEIS, full risk management):
    CAGR=22.0%, Sharpe=1.01, MDD=-22.3%

  Nifty 50 Buy & Hold:
    CAGR=9.8%, Sharpe=0.31, MDD=-38.4%

Conclusion: Base RAAM beats Nifty but loses to V10. Needs crash protection.

PHASE 2: REGIME DETECTOR ABLATION (8 detectors tested)
-------------------------------------------------------
Tested each as a standalone crash gate on the base RAAM system.
(Full results in section 4 above.)

Winner for MDD: nifty_trend (-23.5% vs -35.1% baseline)
Winner for Sharpe: hurst (0.64) - barely better than baseline
Combinations (AND, MAJ) did not outperform solo detectors meaningfully.

Conclusion: Simple nifty_trend is the most effective gate. Complex
statistical detectors (turbulence, absorption, CUSUM) are either too
slow to react or too trigger-happy.

PHASE 3: RANKING FACTOR REPLACEMENT
-------------------------------------
Replaced original Giordano pillars with better-performing alternatives:
  - Dropped Correlation (C) - hurts on single-country universe
  - Replaced Volatility (V) with VolConf (volume confirmation)
  - Added GSADF as third factor (from V10 research)

New ranking: wM*M + wVC*VolConf + wG*GSADF
(Momentum gets the highest weight, VolConf and GSADF split the remainder.)

PHASE 4: ENHANCED ENGINE (STOPS + SECTOR CAPS)
-----------------------------------------------
Added daily trailing stops and sector concentration caps.
Ablated multiple stop levels from tight to loose, plus no-stop baseline.
(Full results in section 6 above.)

Selected the level that balanced MDD protection with trade count.

PHASE 5: RISK-OFF MECHANISM
-----------------------------
Instead of just going to cash (LiquidBees) during risk-off, implemented
momentum-pick between Gold and Cash.

Before (cash only):      Risk-off earns money-market returns
After (gold/cash pick):  Risk-off earns gold returns during gold rallies

This meaningfully improved CAGR during bear markets (2020 COVID crash:
gold rallied while equities crashed).

PHASE 6: SURVIVORSHIP BIAS FIX
-------------------------------
Added delisted tickers. This slightly reduced CAGR (as expected - some
of these stocks were disasters) but made the backtest more honest.

PHASE 7: FINAL INTEGRATION (IKIGAII)
--------------------------------------
Combined all winning components into a single system.
Final result: CAGR=36.6%, Sharpe=1.35, MDD=-33.8%

================================================================================
16. DESIGN DECISIONS & WHY WE MADE THEM
================================================================================

DECISION: Equal weight (not inverse-vol or risk-parity)
  WHY: With only N positions rebalanced monthly, inverse-vol weighting adds
  complexity for marginal benefit. Equal weight is transparent, simple to
  implement live, and avoids concentration in low-vol (often low-return) stocks.

DECISION: Monthly rebalancing (not weekly or bi-monthly)
  WHY: Weekly is too frequent - high turnover and transaction costs dominate.
  Bi-monthly is too slow - you miss sector rotations. Monthly balances
  responsiveness with cost efficiency.

DECISION: Top N positions (not more or fewer)
  WHY: N is concentrated enough to capture alpha from stock selection but
  diversified enough that one stock blow-up does not kill you. With 1/N
  weight, even a -50% single-stock loss is bounded at the portfolio level.
  More positions dilutes alpha. Fewer positions is too concentrated.

DECISION: Long-term SMA for regime (not short-term)
  WHY: A long-term SMA is the gold standard for trend identification. Shorter
  SMAs whipsaw too much in sideways markets. A long-term SMA captures major
  trend changes (bull/bear transitions) without false signals.

DECISION: Use OHLC for stops, not just close prices
  WHY: Close-only stops miss intraday crashes. If a stock opens high, drops
  sharply intraday, and recovers by close, a close-only stop would not trigger.
  But the LOW represents real risk that occurred. Using yesterday low for
  stop checks is more conservative and realistic.

DECISION: Stopped capital to LiquidBees (not re-invested)
  WHY: If a position is stopped out mid-month, reinvesting immediately into
  the next-ranked stock would be:
    1. Chasing - the stop fired because markets are volatile
    2. Extra turnover cost
    3. Hard to implement cleanly (need fresh ranking)
  Parking in LiquidBees until the next scheduled rebal is safer and simpler.

DECISION: Nifty benchmark for regime, not the portfolio own equity curve
  WHY: Using the portfolio own equity for regime detection creates a
  feedback loop (self-referential signal). If the portfolio drops -> go
  risk-off -> portfolio recovers -> go risk-on -> portfolio drops again.
  Using Nifty as an external, independent signal avoids this circularity.

DECISION: Multi-month momentum for Gold/Cash pick
  WHY: Gold tends to trend over multi-month horizons (driven by macro flows:
  rate expectations, USD, risk sentiment). A macro-length lookback captures
  these trends without overfitting to short-term noise.
